How the new shell fits together

The app's frame is changing: a slim rail, drawers that slide in beside your work, one raised white sheet where the work happens, and a side panel for detail. What the app can do is not changing. How it reads the brain, signs you in and runs chats is untouched, because nothing on the server side is edited.

The look is the one you approved in the prototype. This page is about how the pieces connect and where the tricky parts are.

The five layers

Think of a desk with things stacked on it. Each layer sits in front of the one before. The sheet is the only solid white surface, so your work reads as the front layer. The rail and drawers are frosted glass.

What opens what

Rail button

Press To-dos, Chats or Skills on the rail.

The drawer slides in and nudges the sheet over. Only one drawer is open at a time.

Choose something in the drawer

A to-do, a chat or a skill.

The sheet changes. The drawer stays open beside it.

Panel toggle

In the chat's top bar.

The "This chat" panel opens on the right, with Steps and Details tabs.

Cmd+K or Ctrl+K

Or the "Search or jump to" button in the sheet bar.

The palette floats over the app. Up and Down move, Enter runs, Esc closes.
KeyWhat it does
[Widens or narrows the rail.
TTo-dos drawer.
CChats drawer.
SSkills drawer.
Cmd or Ctrl + KCommand palette. Works even while you are typing.
EscCloses the palette first; if it is shut, closes the open drawer. A panel that is pushing stays open; one that is floating closes.

The single letters only work on their own, outside a text box and with the palette shut. Anyone they trip up can switch them off with "Single-key shortcuts" in Settings.

Learn: focus. Focus is where the keyboard is "pointing" right now, the thing that Enter or Space would press. When a drawer opens, focus moves into it, and when it closes, focus goes back to the rail button you used. Without that, keyboard users would lose their place.

When there isn't room

Drawers and the panel normally push the sheet over. On a narrow window that would squeeze your work, so they float over it instead.

Window widthDrawerPanel
Under 900pxFloats. The rail is icons only.Floats.
900 to 1059pxPushes, unless the rail is showing names. Then it floats.Floats.
1060 to 1099pxPushes.Floats.
1100 to 1399pxPushes. Opening it closes the panel.Pushes. Opening it closes the drawer.
1400px and widerPushes.Pushes.

Two rules keep the sheet roomy. On a mid-width window there is space for a drawer or the panel, not both, so whichever you opened last stays and the other closes. And while the panel is open, the rail tucks down to icons and comes back when the panel closes. Your own rail setting is left alone, unless you press the rail button yourself while the panel is open.

A floating drawer or panel behaves like a pop-up. A veil dims the desk behind it, the rest of the app is switched off, and Esc, a click on the veil or choosing a row closes it.

The promise: the sheet never drops below 480px wide. Nothing scrolls sideways, even at 360px.
Learn: breakpoint. A breakpoint is a window width at which the layout changes. The widths in the table are the breakpoints, and they are checked one pixel either side in the tests.

The careful parts

These are the steps most likely to go wrong. Each has a guard in the plan.

Hidden means unreachable

Risk: a closed drawer is invisible, but a keyboard or screen reader could still land in it.

Guard: closed drawers are switched off. So is the whole desk while the palette or a floating drawer is open.

The approval card must never be lost

Risk: a tall card could be cut off, or scroll out of sight behind the message box.

Guard: the card sits in a strip below the thread, not floating over it. It scrolls inside itself past half the sheet's height. After you answer, focus goes to the next card, else the message box.

Esc closes one thing at a time

Risk: one press could cancel the "close chat" question and shut the drawer too.

Guard: the close question marks its Esc as handled, and the app-wide key listener skips anything already handled. The palette closes before the drawer.

A chat from a to-do keeps its link

Risk: moving the wording and helpers around could cut the tie between a chat and its to-do.

Guard: the helper that writes the opening message lives in one shared place, and a test checks it carries the to-do's id. Another test checks a chat started from Home keeps it too.

Learn: inert. Inert means a part of the page is switched off: it can't be clicked, tabbed to or read out by a screen reader. It is how a hidden drawer is made truly unreachable, not just invisible.

Left out on purpose

No Files tab yet

The server only sends file base names, so a file list would be guesswork. It returns when the server sends full paths, which is a separate server change.

No attach button

Home and the chat get no control without a feature behind it.

No app slots on the rail

The framework is there, but nothing shows until a real app exists. Adding one later means adding one entry.

Dark mode later

Light only for now. Dark is a follow-up.

How it gets built

Two waves, so nobody edits the same file at the same time.

Wave 1: the frame

  • Shell state, rail, drawer and panel shells, shared pieces and wording.
  • Removes the old pages, so the app still builds.
  • Ends on a green check before wave 2 starts.

Wave 2: in parallel

  • The three drawers.
  • Home and the command palette.
  • The chat sheet, the "This chat" panel and Settings.

Then the checks

  1. Tests, type-check, lint and build.
  2. Three reviewers: correctness, UI and accessibility, and requirement drift and copy.
  3. An expert UX and accessibility critique against WCAG 2.2 AA.
  4. A browser check on a pretend brain: Home, each drawer, both rail widths, a live chat with each panel tab, a pending approval, the palette, Settings, at 1280, 1000 and 400px, by keyboard only, and with reduced motion.
